# What crop farmers want most from a decision tool: what the surveys say

Research note for FarmSmart. Checked 15 Nov 2026. Every figure below was read on the source page or in the source PDF on that date. Quotes are under 15 words. Where a number could not be confirmed on the source, it is left out.

## Verdict: partly supported

The order **profit > risk > what to plant > pests** is **partly supported**. **Profit comes first** and the evidence is strong. Input costs are the top worry in every Purdue/CME barometer we checked (a record 52% in September 2026). McKinsey finds input prices are the top risk to profit worldwide (48% in 2024; 80% of US farmers in 2022). Unclear return on investment (ROI) and cost are the main reasons farmers don't adopt digital tools. **Risk comes second, but only narrowly, and sometimes it comes first.** McKinsey calls extreme weather "a close second", only 7 points behind input prices, and it is already the top concern in Europe and Latin America. In an open-ended survey of Midwest, Brazilian and Argentine growers, climate was the source of risk named most often, ahead of output and input prices. So "profit" and "risk" are closer to joint first than to 1 and 2, and for many farmers "risk" means weather.

**No survey we found ranks "deciding what to plant" as a separate need.** Crop choice is how farmers act on profit and risk. Field data show acreage follows prices, and rotation benefits pull fields back to the previous crop (Hendricks, Smith and Sumner 2014). **Pests and disease are not reliably fourth.** The big US barometers (Purdue) don't offer them as an answer at all, so their absence there tells us nothing. Where pests are offered, they rank high. In the 2012 Corn Belt survey of 4,778 farmers, about half were concerned about crop disease (50.4%) and insects (49.8%), level with extreme rains and just behind drought (58.5%) and heat stress (52.3%). In Bayer's 2023 eight-country survey, 73% had seen more pest and disease pressure, though crop protection cost came lower as a "top three challenge" (36%). Disease management is the most-used digital agronomy feature in Europe (28%).

A caution on what these surveys measure. Most measure **concerns** (what worries farmers) or **adoption drivers and barriers** (why they buy or skip a tool). Almost none ask which **features** farmers want in a decision tool. The one design study that comes close (Rose et al. 2016) puts **performance, ease of use, trust and cost-benefit** ahead of any topic. So the honest claim is: "surveys show farmers' top worries are costs and profit, then weather and price risk; pests and disease are a major worry where they are asked about." The claim "farmers ranked these four features in this order" is not supported.

## Implications for FarmSmart (front page, plain language)

1. **Lead with money.** First answer on the page: "What would this rotation earn per acre, and what would it cost?" Show input cost next to return, because input costs are the top worry in every US barometer. Label it as a model estimate with its confidence, never as a promise.
2. **Put weather risk right beside money, not below it.** "How does this rotation hold up in a dry year or a wet spring?" Drought and extreme weather are the top or second risk in every survey that offers them. This is where NASA data (soil moisture, rainfall, temperature) earn their place. Label each value with its real grid scale (for example "regional value (≈50 km)").
3. **Show price risk too.** "What if corn prices fall?" McKinsey and the farmdoc survey both rank price swings in the top three. A simple price slider fits here.
4. **Frame "what to plant" as the choice that answers 1 to 3**, not as a separate need: "Compare your current rotation with two alternatives on profit, weather risk and soil." Don't claim farmers ranked "what to plant" as a top feature. No survey asks that.
5. **Keep pests and disease visible, especially as a rotation benefit.** About half of Corn Belt farmers worry about disease and insects. Breaking pest cycles (for example corn rootworm) is a main reason to rotate. Put it in the comparison table, not on the front page.
6. **Earn trust before anything else.** Rose et al. show uptake depends on performance, ease of use, trust and relevance to *this* farm. Show the data source button on every number, say what is missing, keep it to a few clicks, and add "talk this over with your adviser". About half of farmers already use agronomic consultants (Purdue, Oct 2025).
7. **Be honest about ROI.** Unclear ROI is the top barrier to digital tools. Show the cost of switching (equipment, learning, the yield dip in year one) and the payback time, not just the end state.
8. **Wording to avoid:** "Farmers ranked profit, risk, crop choice, then pests." **Wording to use:** "Farmers' top worries are input costs and profit, then weather and price risk (Purdue/CME 2026; McKinsey 2024)."
